# Cotton Crop Production Guide — AgriSaarthi Knowledge Base
# Source: Reference Document based on Central Institute for Cotton Research (CICR) guidelines

## Overview
Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum - Bt Cotton) is the primary cash crop in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.
Sown during Kharif season (June-July). Harvest: November-February.

## Soil Requirements
- Best soil: Deep black cotton soil (vertisols) or red loam
- pH range: 6.0 – 8.0 (ideal: 6.5–7.5)
- Deep, well-drained soils essential
- Avoid waterlogging — cotton is very sensitive to root suffocation
- Minimum depth: 60 cm

## Climate Requirements
- Temperature: 25–35°C (optimal), tolerates up to 40°C
- Rainfall: 500–1500 mm (well-distributed)
- Requires dry weather at harvest

## Popular Bt Cotton Varieties (AP/Telangana)
- Brahma, Tulasi, NHH44, Ankur-2335, Neeraja, JKCHY-1947
- All Bt hybrids contain bollworm resistance gene

## Nutrient Management
General guidelines — verify with soil test:
- Nitrogen: 90–120 kg N/ha
  - Basal: 30%, Side dressing at squaring: 40%, At boll development: 30%
- Phosphorus (P₂O₅): 60–80 kg/ha (full basal)
- Potassium (K₂O): 60–80 kg/ha (full basal or split)
- Micronutrients: Boron (1 kg/ha) important for boll setting
  Zinc deficiency common in AP soils — apply ZnSO₄ 10-15 kg/ha

CAUTION: Excessive nitrogen causes vegetative growth, reducing yield and attracting more pests.

## Pest Management (Critical for Cotton)
1. American Bollworm (Helicoverpa armigera): Attacks squares/bolls
   - Monitor with pheromone traps: 5 moths/trap/night = spray threshold
   - Bt hybrids have tolerance but monitor non-Bt refuge rows
   
2. Pink Bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella): Inside bolls
   - Look for entry/exit holes on bolls
   
3. Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci): Virus vector
   - Yellow sticky traps for monitoring; avoid neonicotinoids in flowering
   
4. Thrips: Silvery streaks on leaves, especially in dry weather
   - More common during early season drought stress

5. Cotton Leafhopper (Jassid): Crinkled/upward curled leaves
   - Use recommended insecticides; avoid spraying during flowering

IMPORTANT: Never apply pesticides without proper identification of pest species.
Contact your local Plant Protection Officer for chemical recommendations.

## Water Management
- Moisture sensitive at: squaring, flowering, boll development
- Drought stress at squaring → flower drop
- Waterlogging at any stage → root rot, Fusarium wilt
- Critical irrigation stages: 30, 60, 90 DAS and boll development

## Harvest Management
- First picking: 160-180 DAS when 60% bolls open
- Subsequent pickings every 15-20 days
- 4-6 pickings typically per season
