# Chilli (Red Chilli) Crop Production Guide — AgriSaarthi Knowledge Base
# Guntur, Andhra Pradesh is India's largest chilli producing district.
# Source: Reference document based on ICAR, ANGRAU guidelines

## Overview
Chilli (Capsicum annuum) is the most important spice crop of Andhra Pradesh.
Guntur chilli is globally famous for its pungency. Grown as Rabi crop (October–March).

## Soil Requirements
- Best: Red sandy loam soils (Guntur), well-drained black soils
- pH: 6.0–7.0 (sensitive to waterlogging)
- NOT suitable: Heavy clay soils, saline soils (EC>2 dS/m)
- Avoid soils with history of Phytophthora root rot

## Climate
- Temperature: 20–30°C for vegetative; 16–25°C for flowering/fruit set
- High humidity (>80%) increases disease pressure (anthracnose, Phytophthora)
- Requires dry weather at harvest for quality
- Frost sensitive — protect seedlings

## Popular Varieties (AP)
- LCA-206, LCA-334 (Guntur local)
- Hybrid varieties: Surya, US-341, Sarpan
- Non-hybrid: Byadagi, Teja (S17 - extra pungent)

## Nursery and Transplanting
- Nursery: Raised beds, sow seeds August–September
- Germination: 7–10 days
- Transplanting age: 35–45 days, 4-6 leaf stage
- Spacing: 60×45 cm or 75×60 cm

## Nutrient Management
General reference (soil test mandatory before applying):
- N: 120–160 kg/ha (split: 30% basal + 30% vegetative + 40% pre-flowering)
- P₂O₅: 80–100 kg/ha (full basal)
- K₂O: 80–100 kg/ha (split: 50% basal + 50% pre-fruiting)
- Ca: Important for preventing blossom end rot
- Boron: 0.5% foliar spray during flowering helps fruit setting
- Magnesium deficiency shows as interveinal chlorosis — apply MgSO₄

## Common Pests and Diseases

### Thrips (Scirtothrips dorsalis)
- Most serious pest; causes leaf curl, silvery appearance
- Transmits Chilli Leaf Curl Virus
- Monitor: Shake branch over white paper, >25 thrips = action threshold
- Blue sticky traps help monitoring

### Mite (Polyphagotarsonemus latus - Broad Mite)
- Downward leaf curl, bronze discoloration
- Appears during hot, dry periods
- Different from thrips curl (compare: thrips=upward curl, mite=downward curl)

### Phytophthora Blight (Phytophthora capsici)
- Causes sudden wilting (damping off in nursery, collar rot/root rot in field)
- Worse in waterlogged conditions
- Prevention: Raised bed transplanting, good drainage, Metalaxyl seed treatment

### Anthracnose / Dieback (Colletotrichum capsici)
- Dark, sunken lesions on fruits (mostly red, ripe fruits)
- Seeds become infected — use certified seeds

### Chilli Leaf Curl Virus (ChiLCV) / Begomovirus
- Transmitted by whitefly
- Symptoms: Upward leaf curl, stunted growth, witches broom
- No cure — remove infected plants, control whitefly vector

## Irrigation
- Chilli sensitive to moisture stress at flowering and fruit setting
- Drip irrigation recommended: 4-5 liters/plant/day during vegetative; more during summer
- Avoid waterlogging — increases Phytophthora risk
- Critical stages: Transplanting, flowering, fruit development

## Harvest and Post-Harvest
- Green chilli: 70-80 DAS
- Red chilli (fresh): 130-150 DAS (when 80% red)
- Dry chilli: Allow further drying to <10% moisture
- Guntur market: Dried red chilli preferred; higher price for uniform color, less blemish
