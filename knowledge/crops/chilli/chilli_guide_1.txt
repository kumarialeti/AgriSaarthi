Chilli Cultivation Guidelines

Climate and Soil
Chilli is a tropical and sub-tropical plant. It grows well in warm and humid climates and dry weather during fruit maturation. A temperature range of 20-25°C is ideal for growth. Frost is detrimental. Well-drained, well-aerated, sandy loam or clay loam soils rich in organic matter are preferred. The optimal soil pH is 6.5 to 7.5.

Nursery Management
Seeds should be treated with beneficial microbes (like Trichoderma) to prevent damping-off disease. Raised beds are recommended for the nursery to prevent water stagnation. Seedlings are ready for transplanting in 4-5 weeks.

Transplanting
Transplant seedlings in the main field at appropriate spacing (e.g., 60x45 cm or 75x60 cm depending on the variety and soil fertility).

Irrigation
Chilli cannot withstand heavy moisture or drought. Frequent and light irrigation is recommended. Drip irrigation is highly efficient and recommended to save water and improve yield.

Harvesting
For green chillies, harvest when fruits are fully mature but still green. For dry chillies, harvest when fruits turn dark red and are slightly wrinkled. Dry the red chillies under the sun until the moisture content is reduced to 10-12%.
